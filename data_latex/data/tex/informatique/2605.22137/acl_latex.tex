\section{Task Tackled}
Multilingual LLMs often express cultural knowledge inconsistently, performing better in local languages than in English \citep{10.1093/pnasnexus/pgae346, zhang-etal-2025-cross}. We address this cross-lingual alignment task, aiming to produce culturally equivalent responses across languages. The system accepts culture-related prompts in a target language or English and generates open-ended or multiple-choice responses aligned to the model's best performance.

\section{Related Works}
Prior work addresses mathematical or coding reasoning performance gap via translation-augmented datasets \citep{chen-etal-2024-breaking} or pre-instruction alignment \citep{zhu-etal-2024-question} to English or cross-lingual consistency for multiple-choice tasks \citep{wang-etal-2025-calm}. We distinguish ourselves by focusing on open-ended culture-related questions and secondly, apply a novel cross-lingual alignment approach.

\section{Approach}
To overcome data scarcity, we propose a self-supervised approach aligning model outputs in local languages and English to the best-performing language's output. 

We investigate three objectives to align the target language representation to the ground truth: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) \citep{ouyang2022training}, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) \citep{rafailov2023direct}, and Generative Reinforcement Preference Optimization (GRPO) \citep{shao2024deepseekmath}.

\section{Dataset}
We construct training data using \textbf{CANDLE} \citep{nguyen2023extracting} (1.1M assertions) as seeds. We employ GPT-4o to convert assertions into open-ended questions, translating them into the culture's local language via Google Translate to create parallel queries. We evaluate alignment on the \textbf{BLEnD} benchmark \citep{myung2024blend}, measuring knowledge transfer accuracy across linguistic representations.
